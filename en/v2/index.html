The value is not a product, it is a way of approaching problems: looking at where football decides out of habit and building a method anyone can check.

The problem

When a team loses a player, the usual search is for someone in the same role. But a role describes a position on the whiteboard, not what the player actually did on the pitch.

The Theseus paradox

If you replace one plank at a time, is it still the same ship? Applied to a team: it stays the same when the same tasks and relationships between players remain, not the same names.

From role to task

In relational football players do not hold fixed roles: they carry out tasks by zone and context. Who moves close to the ball carrier, who gives width, who attacks depth, who covers the lost ball. A team's identity lives in this network of tasks.

RoleTasks by zone and context
Central midfielderIn low build-up: drops close to the ball carrier
In the final third: occupies the space between the lines
On losing the ball: among the first to press

The method

  1. Map a team's tasks by zone and phase, from video and event data.
  2. Describe the outgoing player as a set of tasks, not a role.
  3. Look for candidates who perform those tasks in similar contexts.
  4. The coach validates: data proposes, the pitch decides.

A teaching demo on free public data is in preparation: every calculation will be reproducible, with public dataset and scripts.

Limits

  • Public event data is incomplete.
  • Context matters: the same action means different things in different teams.
  • The method supports the decision, it does not replace it.
